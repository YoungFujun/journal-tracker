Social Ties and Fiscal Transfers in a Regional Economy: A Quantitative Spatial Analysis
Yuwan Duan, Yatang Lin, Yi Lu
2026-09-08 | Full article
Abstract Many governments use fiscal transfers to reduce regional inequality, yet discretionary allocation may also depend on informal communication among officials. We examine this mechanism in a large central-to-provincial transfer system, using policymakers’ shared workplace, alumni, and hometown backgrounds to measure social ties. Exploiting ministerial turnover and controlling for provincial leadership, we find that prior workplace ties significantly increase transfers. We then embed these estimates in a quantitative spatial model with trade, migration, input-output linkages, and fiscal-productivity effects. Social ties redirect resources toward richer provinces with stronger fundamentals, raising welfare and output while weakening redistribution. Under egalitarian weights, however, removing ties improves welfare.
The Intergenerational Transmission of Employers and the Earnings of Young Workers
Matthew Staiger
2026-09-08 | Full article
Abstract This paper investigates how parental connections to firms shape early-career earnings. I use employer-employee linked data to study one important type of connection: jobs obtained at a parent’s employer. Twenty-nine percent of individuals work for a parent’s employer at least once by age 30. Exploiting transitory, firm-specific fluctuations in hiring conditions at the parent’s employer, I estimate that working for a parent’s employer increases initial earnings by 24 percent, primarily by providing access to higher-paying firms. Individuals with higher-earning parents are more likely to work for a parent’s employer and experience larger earnings gains when they do. Consequently, the elasticity of initial earnings with respect to parental earnings would be 12 percent lower if no one found a job through these connections. The findings suggest that connections to firms through one’s social network may be an important determinant of intergenerational mobility.
Return to Office and the Tenure Distribution
David Van Dijcke, Florian Gunsilius, Austin Wright
2026-09-08 | Full article
Abstract Debates over return-to-office mandates have intensified since the COVID-19 pandemic ended, though their economic implications remain poorly understood. Using large-scale resume data, we analyze how these policies affect employee tenure and seniority at three large U.S. tech companies: Microsoft, SpaceX, and Apple. Employing a distributional synthetic controls framework, we estimate a reduction in tenure and seniority at firms returning to the office. We extend the framework with a functional bootstrap procedure and prove its validity for constructing uniform confidence bands. Transaction Costs and the Gains from Trade in Water Markets
Nick Hagerty
2026-09-08 | Full article
Abstract Surface water in California is scarce and tightly managed, yet little is traded through a market. This paper studies transaction costs in the existing wholesale water market and estimates the benefits of reducing them. I develop an empirical framework that uses transactions among water districts to analyze welfare, decomposing prices into demand and transaction costs and simulating a market with only physical frictions. 2026-09-08 | Full article
Abstract We quantify the benefits of better firm-to-firm matching in an aggregate diffusion model where individuals acquire profitable knowledge from others. Abstract We measure which economic topics are top of mind using quarterly German household and firm panels from 2020 to 2024, a period that spans the post-pandemic inflation surge and the subsequent disinflation.
